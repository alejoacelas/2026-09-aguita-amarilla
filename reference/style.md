# Style: the "half-assed MS Paint" look of this video

Bottom line: flat Windows 7 Paint drawings made with the default 20-colour palette, a 5 px black freehand brush, clean ellipse and rectangle tools for objects, bucket fills and the spray-can tool for anything soft. Windows Movie Maker-style effects (spinning card, plain title cards) join them into a slideshow. One base scene (the peeing stick figure) is reused 43 times with only the speech bubble swapped.

## Canvas

- The video is 960x720 (4:3). The drawing sits in a letterbox band 960x414 at y=154, so the image is about 2.32:1. A drawn circle (the Earth, frame_040) measures 275x275 px, so the drawings were not stretched: the canvas really is that wide and short.
- Almost every slide uses a pure white #FFFFFF background. Coloured backgrounds come from a bucket fill of the whole canvas (sky, sea, grey street, yellow title card).

## Line quality

- Freehand strokes are a round brush about 4-6 px wide on the 960 px frame, drawn with a mouse: wobbly, with visible direction changes, strokes that overshoot or fail to meet at joints, and letters with uneven sizes.
- Objects that should be round or boxy use the shape tools: the toilet is two clean ellipses plus a curved bowl, the sun is a perfect ellipse, the washing machine and buildings are rectangles with rectangle windows, and the arrows are polygon-tool block arrows. That mix of shaky freehand people with geometrically perfect props defines the look.
- Speech bubbles are one freehand loop with a pointed tail toward the figure's head, drawn in one or two strokes.

## Fills

- Solid regions are flat bucket fills inside a black outline (sun, sand, fields, trees, buildings, town hall). There is no shading or gradient.
- "Soft" things use the spray-can tool: clouds and water vapour are speckled yellow/blue airbrush smears, the pee in the river and sea is a faint yellow airbrush patch, and rain on the school and town hall is a few sprayed dots plus short yellow and blue strokes.
- Rain is short diagonal strokes alternating #FFF200 yellow and blue, a visual joke that the rain is the pee.

## Text

- Most words are mouse-written capitals in the same 5 px black brush: "JAJAJA JAJA", "MI AGUITA AMARILLA", "HUM HUM", "EL PASTOR", "LAS VAQUIÑAS", "LOS CAMPOS", "TU CASA", "TU FAMILIA", often with a hand-drawn arrow to the labelled object.
- A few labels use Paint's text tool in a small plain font, deliberately inconsistent with the rest: "Cálida y tibia", "la la la la...", "Tu lugar de trabajo", "LAS CALLES", "EL AYUNTAMIENTO".
- The one designed card (frame_042) is bold black Comic Sans "MI AGUITA AMARILLA" on a solid yellow canvas.
- The title and credits (frames 001, 044) are video-editor text on black, grey and white sans-serif.

## Characters and recurring elements

- The narrator: a stick figure with a lumpy oval head, dot eyes, a curved smile, a stick body, two straight arms held out, and a solid black blob at the crotch from which the yellow stream arcs into an ellipse-tool toilet on the right. The figure, toilet and stream stay pixel-identical across 43 of the 77 slide appearances; only the bubble text or the mouth changes (open mouth for laughing, frown for "MMH...").
- The intro figure: a smaller stick figure with a single cowlick hair loop, shown spinning on a rotating white card.
- Background sets are reused while small props are added: one river frame gains the shepherd, then the cows; one beach frame gains fish, a squid, a jellyfish, a hake.
- Animals are simple outline blobs with a few leg sticks, such as the cows with irregular black spots and pink udders, and the fish drawn as outline ovals with a triangle tail.
- There is one pasted photograph (a beer mug, frame_003) placed next to hand-drawn "X 40". Its realism is part of the joke.

## Pacing

Slides are hard cuts timed to the lyric, usually 1.5-4 s each. One image illustrates each noun in the lyric (house, family, river, shepherd, cows, fields, fish, squid, jellyfish, hake, sun, streets, father, mother, school, town hall, world). The only motion is the editor's spin effect on the intro figure (0:05-0:33 and 4:17-4:22).
